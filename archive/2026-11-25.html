先问“反过来会怎样”：一个简单的思考习惯

Date: 2026-11-25 | Level: B1 (target) | Topic: Critical thinking and decision-making — testing a decision by asking what would happen if the opposite were true

读的时候，注意作者怎样用一个简单问题来检查自己的想法；读完后，试着用同样的问题检查你今天的一个小决定。

When we make a decision, we often look only for reasons that support it. We feel good about a choice, so we collect facts that agree with us. This feels natural, but it can lead us to a weak decision. A simple habit can help. Before you decide, ask one question: "What if the opposite were true?" For example, you want to buy a new phone. You tell yourself it will make you happy. Now turn it around: what if it does not make you happy? Then you may see that your old phone still works well.

当我们做决定时，常常只寻找支持这个决定的理由。我们对一个选择感觉良好，于是就收集同意我们的事实。这感觉很自然，但它可能导致一个不牢靠的决定。一个简单的习惯可以帮助我们。在做决定之前，问一个问题：“如果反过来会怎样？”例如，你想买一部新手机。你告诉自己它会让你开心。现在反过来想：如果它并不能让你开心呢？那么你可能会发现，你的旧手机其实还能用得很好。

This question works because it makes us look for missing information. Our first idea is often only one part of the picture. Asking "what if the opposite were true?" pushes us to find the other part. A student may think, "I should study all night before the exam." Turn it around: what if studying all night is a bad idea? Then she remembers that she cannot think clearly when she is tired. So she sleeps for seven hours and studies in the morning. The question did not give her the answer, but it helped her see the whole situation.

这个问题有效，是因为它让我们去寻找缺失的信息。我们的第一个想法往往只是全貌的一部分。问“如果反过来会怎样？”会推动我们找到另一部分。一个学生可能想：“考试前我应该通宵学习。”反过来想：如果通宵学习是个坏主意呢？于是她想起，自己累的时候无法清楚地思考。所以她睡了七个小时，早上再学习。这个问题没有给她答案，但帮助她看到了整个情况。

This habit is not about being negative. It is about being fair to both sides. You do not need to change your mind every time. Sometimes, after you ask the question, you still choose the same thing. That is fine. Now you know your choice is stronger, because you have tested it. The key is to ask before you act, not after. Try it with small things first, like what to eat or which bus to take. With practice, the question becomes easy, and your decisions become clearer.

这个习惯并不是要变得消极。它是为了公平地对待双方。你不需要每次都改变主意。有时候，问完这个问题后，你仍然选择同样的东西。这没关系。现在你知道你的选择更牢固了，因为你已经检验过它。关键是在行动之前问，而不是之后。先从小事开始尝试，比如吃什么或坐哪路公交车。通过练习，这个问题会变得容易，你的决定也会变得更清晰。
